The Performance Gap Between Architectures
One of the report's most striking findings is that the basic RAG pipeline consistently and substantially outperforms the advanced agentic pipeline across context precision, context recall, and answer correctness, while also delivering significantly lower latency. The authors provide a reasonable explanation centered on error accumulation across sequential LLM calls in the advanced pipeline and the limited reasoning capacity of sub-10B parameter models. At present, the discussion notes that the advanced system "pulled in a lot of irrelevant content" and that "the reranking stage discarded useful context, or the multi-step retrieval introduced additional noise." These are distinct failure modes with different solutions. Clarifying whether the performance degradation is due to the query decomposition step generating poor sub-questions, the retrieval step returning low-quality results for those sub-questions, the reranking step discarding relevant material, or the synthesis step failing to integrate sub-answers would provide more actionable guidance for future iterations. If the bottleneck is the small model's inability to execute the meta-reasoning tasks required by the agentic architecture, the recommendation section's emphasis on prompt engineering and fine-tuning is supported. If the bottleneck is in the chunking and retrieval of context, then investing in those components may yield greater returns than improving the LLM's reasoning.
